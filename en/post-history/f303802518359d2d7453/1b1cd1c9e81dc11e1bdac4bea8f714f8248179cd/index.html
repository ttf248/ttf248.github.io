City closure

I had very little memory of SARS as a child, having experienced it back then. Later, when I saw related information, I realized that it ended before widespread diffusion occurred because the incubation period was relatively short. I remember being in elementary school at the time, and we were dismissed early every day. The classroom always smelled of disinfectant.

It’s been almost three years since the COVID-19 pandemic started in late 2019. People working outside their hometowns have gotten used to it, wearing masks when necessary. This wave of the epidemic in Shanghai initially originated from Hong Kong and spread to Shenzhen through cross-border entry points. The situation here in Shanghai was also caused by a series of imported cases from Hong Kong. The authorities finally announced that the outbreak was due to inadequate protective measures at quarantine hotels, leading to the spread of the virus after mutation. While the toxicity of the mutated virus has weakened, its transmission speed has increased through the hotel’s ventilation system. Initially, it wasn’t serious and could have been contained.

People are always confident. The leaders in Shanghai are the same. They will choose us for our gridded risk control, precise prevention and control.

The results are now visible to everyone; the new cases have accumulated beyond 20,000. Driven to desperation, they chose a lockdown, but avoided using the term “lockdown” externally, as they had previously stated in press conferences that Shanghai did not need a lockdown, preserving what little face they had left.

Buy groceries

The food delivery industry is a new sector created by the internet. The core of it is that you need someone to deliver for you, right? But due to widespread lockdowns caused by the pandemic, businesses could operate, but there was no one to make deliveries – the entire chain lacked its final link. People outside might not understand why everyone in Shanghai, an international metropolis, suddenly rushed to buy groceries. But if you think about it, it makes sense. Most people are from out of town, working and living in rented apartments. They usually eat at company cafeterias or restaurants, rarely cooking at home. When the usual routes were blocked, those who could afford it started buying groceries. Because this lockdown announcement wasn’t given in advance, most people didn’t have a significant stockpile of food or vegetables. This led to the rush seen in the videos, and that kind of gathering directly caused another wave of infections.

Industry

Everyone I know works in the IT industry. The pandemic affected me, and I experienced working from home. Back in 2019, I was stuck at home for almost a month, rescheduling train tickets countless times, unable to determine when I could return to Shenzhen. It’s unimaginable how people in the catering, tourism, or many service industries have been getting by these past few years.
