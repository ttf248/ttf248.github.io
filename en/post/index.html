Big cities can make money, but they turn couples into long-distance relationships

Sep 18, 2026

Big cities offer opportunities to make money, while hometowns do not. The starting point for many couples living apart is not a problem with their relationship, but rather the reality both face: income opportunities are concentrated in big cities, and they want to stay together, yet they lack sufficient housing and financial breathing room.

When we were in school, our imagination of the future was often simple: leave work on time, go home and learn something we’re interested in, and go out for a walk on weekends. Few people imagined that later they wouldn’t be able to afford a house, would have to work overtime every day, or would even be too lazy to move after coming home from work or on weekends. When making money and living must be chosen between, marriage is easily arranged into two cities first.

This message can most easily be read as a simple product choice: NVIDIA ditched Claude and GPT in favor of its in-house Nemotron. A more accurate reading is that when the most powerful models begin to require data retention across requests and sessions for safety defense, enterprises finally discover that “not using it for training” and “not letting the vendor keep it” are fundamentally not the same thing.

As of September 15, 2026, the conclusions that can be supported by public reporting are not that dramatic either: NVIDIA has been reported to restrict Anthropic’s Fable to lower-sensitivity tasks, using Nemotron for sensitive internal supply chain projects; reports have placed OpenAI on a broader enterprise customer review list, but no formal notice of an “across-the-board ban of Anthropic and OpenAI frontier models by NVIDIA” has been made public. Family Planning Thinned Out Clans, But It Didn't Decide Who Could Become a Tycoon

Sep 09, 2026

When you put family planning, public officials, and American plutocratic families together in the same question, it’s easy to arrive at a smooth-sounding answer: fewer children make it harder for great families to form; China has no Rockefellers, but America does, because America doesn’t strictly restrict births. This answer compresses three different problems into one.

Family planning did thin out the horizontal branches of families, especially the networks of siblings and cousins; but it alone cannot explain why a country did not develop wealth dynasties of the American kind. For a family to evolve from “many relatives” to “sustained control of wealth or power” also requires property rights, corporations, inheritance, and organizational relationships. Population policy affects how many nodes a family has; institutions determine whether those nodes hold assets that can be passed down.

Stainless Steel Is Not Just About 304; Standards Are the Easiest Point of Confusion in Online Purchase Disputes

Sep 01, 2026

A Zhihu topic discussion went like this: someone bought a stainless steel item on Taobao that seemed to not match its advertised specifications, and got into a back-and-forth dispute with the seller. In the comment section, several familiar suggestions quickly appeared: buy from a brand, go to JD.com, or go to Pinduoduo—at least those platforms have customer service; others provided a list of standard numbers, saying don’t just ask about 304 and 316, ask about GB 4806.9 and GB/T 29601.

Half of this kind of advice is correct, but the other half muddles together four different things into one answer: steel grade, food-contact safety, product performance standards, and the path to liability when disputes arise. A genuinely useful buying approach isn’t memorizing some “customer service code phrase,” but knowing exactly what question each standard is answering.

From Coupons to Season Passes: Player Migration in Tencent's Earnings Reports

Aug 27, 2026

If you only look at a single day’s bestseller chart, the gaming industry looks like a track where the runners keep changing: today it’s MOBA, tomorrow it’s shooters, and the day after that a new strategy or RPG emerges. But when you line up Tencent’s fifteen years of financial reports, a different picture emerges: genres haven’t simply replaced one another—the entry points, life cycles, and monetization triggers of products are stratified.

This is also why Tencent is suitable as a “weather vane for the gaming industry,” but cannot serve as a complete industry dashboard. Its financial reports are long enough to show the shifting gears between PC clients, mobile, domestic, and overseas markets; however, it does not fully break down annual revenue by categories such as “how much MOBA games earned, how much shooter games earned.” Apple’s China App Store top-grossing games page, on the other hand, is like a display window—it reveals which products on a particular platform at a particular moment are still compelling enough for users to pay for them, but it cannot replace comprehensive market statistics.
