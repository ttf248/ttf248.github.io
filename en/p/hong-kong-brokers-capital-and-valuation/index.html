Hong Kong Brokers Will Diverge Rather Than Re-rate Together

Platform clients, control, and returns on capital explain why Hong Kong brokers will not all command higher valuations

Aug 12, 2026 · 6 minute read · Codex (GPT-5) · v8.11.1
中文 日本語
Category
  • AI Investment
AI writing

One brokerage raises capital, another is acquired by a technology company, and a third prepares to leave the stock market. Seen against the same Hong Kong market rally, these developments can look like the prelude to an industry-wide re-rating. My question is more specific: once the money goes in, which business can deliver a better return?

As of August 12, 2026, an Ant-affiliated buyer had completed its controlling acquisition of Bright Smart Securities, CITIC Securities had completed a targeted H-share issue, and an offeror under Guotai Haitong had proposed taking Guotai Junan International private. The third remains a proposal subject to preconditions; these cannot be described as three completed acquisitions.

Each action buys a different capability: a local operating base, an offshore balance sheet, or control within a group. A capital transaction can change a brokerage’s starting point. A higher valuation still requires evidence that the added capital can consistently earn more than its cost.

Why raise more money when markets are busy?

HKEX reported average daily cash-market turnover of HK$283.0 billion in the first half of 2026, up 17.8% year on year; IPO proceeds of HK$210.2 billion, up 92.1%; and southbound average daily turnover of HK$123.1 billion. Recovering trading and fundraising activity creates opportunities for brokers, but not all of them generate commission income with little capital tied up.

Margin loans require funding up front. Derivatives and market making consume capital. Prime brokerage needs financing, clearing, and risk management. As markets become busier and clients demand more of these services, brokers may reach the limits of their balance sheets first. Moving online can reduce some acquisition and branch costs; it does not eliminate spending on anti-money-laundering controls, suitability, system stability, or cross-market settlement.

CITIC Securities completed an issue of approximately 803.7 million H shares on August 6, raising net proceeds of about HK$18.413 billion to remain offshore for international business development. Previously identified uses included capital-intermediation businesses such as equity derivatives, commodities, and fixed income. The money buys capacity to meet more client needs, not merely another trading interface.

But more capital also enlarges the equity denominator. If profits do not keep up, a bigger business can produce a lower ROE. The first test after a capital increase is therefore not whether trading volume rises, but whether the added business warrants the capital it consumes after funding costs, risks, and operating investment.

A discount to book needs an explanation, not just a wait

In a simplified residual-income model with stable growth:

$$ \frac{P}{B}=\frac{ROE-g}{r-g} $$

Here, ROE is sustainable return on equity, g is the long-term growth rate, and r is the cost of equity. The model requires its assumptions about growth, returns, and capital costs to hold, with r > g. Only when growth is set to zero do we obtain the approximation PB = ROE / r.

If long-term ROE is 5% while shareholders require 10%, a price-to-book ratio of 0.5 under zero growth is not hard to understand. This is a hypothetical illustration, not a valuation of a particular company. Asset quality, earnings volatility, distributions, and funding costs can all make actual prices differ from the simplified model.

A low valuation may mean the market has not believed a company’s improvement. It may also mean the market has correctly recognized its weak returns. Operating results must distinguish the two. In particular, a premium in a control transaction cannot simply be transferred to every small listed broker.

The proposed privatization price for Guotai Junan International is HK$3 per share, a 44.2% premium to the closing price before suspension. Multiplying by roughly 9.530 billion shares gives an estimated total equity value of HK$28.59 billion. Against approximately HK$15.859 billion of shareholders’ equity at the end of 2025, that is a static price-to-book ratio of about 1.8. The parent already owns a majority, so total equity value is not the cash the offeror would actually pay.

This is not a typical purchase of net assets at half price. A controlling owner may also consider group synergies, internal resource allocation, and the cost of maintaining a listed platform. Minority shareholders face a different set of rights and exit conditions. A premium for control of one company does not give every low-PB peer the same scope for revaluation.

Ant is buying time; winning clients remains another task

The Ant-affiliated buyer purchased about 50.55% of Bright Smart for approximately HK$2.814 billion, completing the transaction on March 30, 2026. After the general offer closed, the buyer and its concert parties held about 50.63%.

For someone building a Hong Kong brokerage from scratch, the license is only the beginning. Account opening, trading, financing, clearing, customer service, complaints, and compliance must work together before clients will consider transferring assets. Buying an operating brokerage can shorten the time needed to build these capabilities.

What is harder to buy is the client’s next decision. Active users of a payment platform do not automatically become funded brokerage clients. Opening a securities account does not mean transferring long-term assets into it. Crossing both gaps depends on suitable products, transferred trust, continuity of service, and the regulatory conditions governing cross-border solicitation.

After the acquisition, the useful measures go beyond a new logo, a redesigned app, or subsidies. Net client inflows, retention, use of wealth products, and service costs matter more. If these do not change, the business may remain a traditional brokerage with a stronger shareholder.

For Futu, assets kept in accounts matter more than apps installed on phones

Futu disclosed approximately 3.59 million funded accounts and HK$1.22 trillion in client assets at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Quarterly trading volume was about HK$4.15 trillion, with wealth-management assets of about HK$178.4 billion. Of roughly HK$5.9 billion in revenue, brokerage commissions and handling charges contributed about HK$2.64 billion, and interest income about HK$2.65 billion.

These measures need to remain separate. Client assets are a stock at a particular date; trading volume is a quarterly flow; revenue is what the platform earns. Frequent trading does not necessarily imply long-term asset retention. Rising client assets can also reflect market appreciation, so not all of the increase is net inflow.

The figures nevertheless reveal a platform structure. Beyond trading, an account supports cash, financing, funds, options, and other services. When clients do not need to move their assets elsewhere frequently, the platform has more opportunities to serve them. A larger product range and asset base may also spread system and acquisition costs. This explains a mechanism; the financial report does not establish every causal relationship between products.

New entrants can copy screens and commission schedules more quickly than they can replicate client trust, asset retention, and service across products. Futu’s scale also has limits. On May 27, 2026, the China Securities Regulatory Commission issued advance notices of administrative penalties to relevant Futu, Tiger, and Longbridge entities concerning unlawful cross-border business. That announcement alone does not establish when later proceedings concluded, but it underlines the specific regulatory constraints on cross-border acquisition of clients.

Not every challenger needs to become Futu

Ranking Hong Kong brokers as if they were only apps misses the business differences that determine competition.

Path What it seeks A test that is easily overlooked
Platform businesses such as Futu, Tiger, and Longbridge Funded clients, retention, and use of multiple products Whether net inflows persist, client sourcing is lawful, and interest and commission income are sensitive to the cycle
Technology ecosystems reshaping traditional brokers Combining an existing local organization with new products Whether old and new systems, clients, and compliance processes genuinely connect
Comprehensive financial groups Cross-border business for institutions, companies, and affluent clients Funding costs, risk-adjusted returns on capital, and international service capability
Small specialist brokers Particular client groups, teams, or specialist services Whether differentiation covers fixed costs, and assets and clients can be retained after a transaction

These paths overlap, but one new-account metric cannot assess them all. A comprehensive group need not beat Futu at every retail feature. Institutional relationships, derivatives, or corporate financing may provide its returns. Smaller brokers need not always sell themselves either; specialization may be more realistic than trying to offer every product.

Nor is acquisition an exit available to every small company. Buyers will consider whether clients can migrate, teams will stay, systems are compatible, and assets are clean. A license and a balance-sheet equity figure may not outweigh the cost of integration.

I would therefore ground an industry view in changes that can be tracked over time: how much client-asset growth comes from net inflows; whether revenue depends too heavily on market activity and interest rates; whether new capital improves risk-adjusted returns; and whether client sourcing meets each jurisdiction’s rules. Improvement in these measures supports a case for higher valuations. Financing, acquisitions, and turnover rising together are not enough to declare an industry-wide re-rating.

Hong Kong brokers are more likely to diverge along these lines of capability. Capital is already choosing where to go. Operating performance now has to justify those choices.

This article is industry analysis, not investment advice.
