From Invoices to Offshore Trusts: Two Decades of Change in China’s Tax Administration

The focus has expanded from factories, transactions and wages to tax status, relationships and income from overseas assets

Aug 27, 2026 · 7 minute read · Codex (GPT-5) · v8.11.1
中文 日本語
Category
  • AI Finance Knowledge Base
AI writing

The July 2026 announcement on offshore-trust taxation brought a wealth arrangement that can seem remote from everyday life down to specific questions: who contributed the assets, who earned the income, which year requires a filing, and what records establish the facts?

It does not mean China’s tax authorities are taking over overseas trustees, or that every overseas financial product suddenly faces a new tax. It extends a direction visible in two decades of administrative reform: where assets are held is no longer sufficient on its own to explain the tax relationship. Tax residence, actual control and the attribution of income also matter.

Tax reform does not always aim to collect more

Looking back to 2006, the first conspicuous change was a withdrawal: the agricultural tax was abolished nationwide, and rural tax-and-fee reform moved further toward comprehensive reform. Reform can end taxation of a category of activity or redistribute burdens. It should not be equated with higher rates from the outset.

Individual income tax also changed that year, with an adjustment to the employment-income expense deduction and expanded self-reporting and reporting covering all employees and their income. The first altered the burden; the second altered the information available. The question was not only how much one employer withheld, but what income an individual received.

The Enterprise Income Tax Law took effect in 2008, bringing domestic and foreign-invested enterprises into a unified framework for rates, deductions and preferences. This changed the relationship between corporate status and the tax system; it was not simply a matter of collecting more by auditing a few more accounts.

These reforms show that administrative capability depends on the tax rules it administers. First decide what is taxable and who bears the obligation, then consider how to identify it. Seeing more clearly does not mean everyone must pay more.

After the VAT reform, the same category could describe different activity

Measured in units of RMB 100 million, national public-finance tax revenue in 2012 was 100,614.28, including VAT of 26,415.51 and business tax of 15,747.64. The business-tax-to-VAT reform was extended comprehensively in 2016. In the same units, that year VAT revenue rose to 40,712, while business tax fell to 11,502.

Looking only at VAT growth could suggest a comparable increase in economic activity. In fact, construction, real estate, finance and consumer services that had previously entered fiscal accounts through business tax began entering through VAT, alongside input deductions and policy-driven revenue reductions. Activity moved between tax categories. Growth in one category cannot all be counted as a larger tax base.

The deeper change concerned transaction chains. Upstream documents, downstream invoicing and input-tax deductions became connected. Understanding one company increasingly required understanding its transactions with others. An invoice became not only evidence of an individual receipt but also information linking business relationships.

Merging collection agencies did not merge every fiscal account

The 2015 reform of the national and local tax administration systems emphasized coordination in services, enforcement and information. In 2018, national and local tax agencies merged at provincial and lower levels, while tax authorities’ responsibilities for collecting non-tax revenue and social-insurance contributions also expanded.

Collection by the same agency does not make receipts identical in nature. Social-insurance funds, government-managed funds, non-tax revenue and taxes must still be examined separately. Otherwise, a change in the collecting agency can be mistaken for a change in the structure of taxation.

Cross-border information collection advanced as well. China introduced financial-account due diligence under CRS and first exchanged information internationally in 2018. It helps identify account holders, controlling persons and tax residence, but CRS is an information framework, not a separate tax. Whether reported information leads to a tax liability still depends on substantive rules such as income-tax law.

The Individual Income Tax Law, amended in 2018 and effective in 2019, changed the residence criteria and introduced individual anti-avoidance provisions. Residents’ obligations concerning overseas income did not begin with that amendment. The new arrangements further addressed residence, overseas income and unreasonable tax-avoidance arrangements.

The 2021 reform guidelines called for electronic invoices and tax data to support more differentiated, targeted supervision. That was a direction for development, not proof that every dataset was already connected or every risk identified. Identifying relationships, verifying evidence and enforcing the law remain separate tasks.

Revenue growth alone does not measure administrative efficiency

When discussing government tax receipts, I focus on tax revenue within the national general public budget and distinguish other receipts. All amounts below are nominal, in RMB 100 million. Historical changes in tax rules and budget classifications also matter.

Taxes remain the main component of the general public budget, but not the whole of it. Non-tax revenue grew 25.4% in 2024. In 2025, total general public budget revenue fell while tax revenue increased slightly. The total alone hides the change in composition.

Refunds also affect the recorded figures. Tax revenue fell 3.5% year on year in 2022 but increased 6.6% after excluding the effect of VAT credit refunds. Two treatments produce opposite directions for the same year: policy and cash-flow timing cannot be left out of an interpretation of tax receipts.

Land revenue belongs to another account. Revenue from land-use-right transfers was approximately RMB 4,151.8 billion in 2025. In the first seven months of 2026 it fell 30.8% year on year, while general public budget tax revenue increased 6.7%. Both can happen because they record different economic activities and belong to different budget categories. Individual income tax rose 14.9% over the same period; that increase alone cannot reveal how much any new administrative measure contributed.

Economic scale, prices, profits, rates, refunds, asset transactions and collection all affect revenue. Identifying the effect of one reform requires more evidence than a total. Rising receipts do not by themselves prove stronger administration, and falling receipts do not by themselves prove its failure.

Offshore trusts: the parties and the stages matter

Announcement No. 21 of 2026, issued by the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration, covers overseas trusts and arrangements with similar functions, with exclusions for qualifying regulated public financial products. It does not classify all overseas funds and insurance products as offshore trusts.

The rules cannot be summarized as “the beneficiary is a Chinese resident.” Where a resident individual contributes assets to a trust, income during the trust’s existence is in principle reported annually by that contributor; actual distribution is not the sole trigger. Where a nonresident contributes assets and distributions are then made to a resident, another set of attribution rules applies. Actual control and contributions by multiple parties can also affect the assessment. Contribution, ongoing income and distribution must be considered together with the status of the parties. Announcement No. 21

Administrative Announcement No. 15 translates those relationships into filing and documentation requirements, distinguishing contributors, beneficiaries and different events. Trust agreements, asset schedules, organizational structures and financial records allow tax authorities to compare the legal arrangement with the actual attribution of income. This is more precise than simply saying offshore trusts are no longer secret. Administrative announcement

Transitional filing arrangements do not erase historical tax liabilities. The 90-day window has specific conditions. It cannot be read as an automatic exemption for earlier years or a prohibition on lawful recovery of tax. A particular arrangement still requires examination of the full announcements, tax residence, assets and income. An institutional overview cannot replace an assessment of an individual case.

Neither announcement provides a forecast of additional fiscal revenue. Better identification of an arrangement does not mean a corresponding amount of tax will necessarily arrive in the next year. Valuations, income classification, foreign tax already paid and the underlying attribution of income still need to be established.

The next test is whether relationships can be supported by evidence

The last two decades do not form a straight line of rising tax rates. Agricultural tax disappeared; unification and VAT reform changed the tax categories through which activity was recorded; agency mergers changed the organization; cross-border information and electronic invoicing changed what could be identified. Each adjusted a different part of the same question: who owes what because of which economic activity, and how can compliance be demonstrated?

Offshore trusts extend that work to more complex asset arrangements. Rules can define controlling persons and income attribution, but daily administration still needs sufficient evidence, treatment of valuation and cross-border differences, and consistent enforcement. The issue worth watching is not only how much more money the government collects. It is whether asset relationships can be identified more accurately, and whether taxpayers can know their obligations and the basis for filing in advance.
